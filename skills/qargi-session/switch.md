# The lead moves to another runtime

The lead skills are installed for each runtime, and the marshal records, the runs and the project's answers are qargi's, not a runtime's: a lead on either reads the same state. The procedure of `SKILL.md` holds, with these steps.

1. **You write the note** (steps 1 and 2 there): `qargi marshal handoff --write FILE`. Under `Lead's addition (not stored state)` name your runtime and the identity you led under. The note's packs section and launch line describe the project's local runtime, which need not be the incoming lead's.
2. **The owner starts the incoming session** in the work tree where the work continues, the way its runtime starts one, with the note's path as the first message.
3. **The incoming lead reads the note and reports before it does anything** (step 4 there), adding its runtime, the identity in its `QARGI_LEAD` and where that comes from, whether a session-start hook ran, and the lead skills it sees. Until it is told to proceed it runs only verbs that read, such as `qargi marshal status --all` and `qargi marshal check NAME`.
4. **The report is checked against the stored state**, by you or by the owner: `qargi marshal status --all` now, a fresh `qargi marshal handoff` when the work moved, then the four failure points of step 5 there.
5. **Then each feature changes hands.** You run `qargi marshal release NAME --note "handoff note: FILE"` and nothing on the feature after it: it has no lead until the take, so no flow verb is refused. The incoming lead runs `qargi marshal take NAME` with its identity in `QARGI_LEAD`, never with `--as` alone. When your session ended without a release, the incoming lead runs the note's `next lead` line, `qargi marshal claim NAME --as-successor-of ID`: it is accepted only once your lease is stale, and only on the same host, as the same OS user, in the same work tree; the runtime plays no part. Every other case is the owner's `qargi marshal takeover`. The incoming lead ends with `qargi marshal check NAME`, which must answer `yours`.

## What differs between runtimes

- **Identity.** A lead is the id in `QARGI_LEAD`; with none it is the anonymous user@host, which every such session of that user on the host shares. Two lead sessions on one host need distinct ids, or the marshal answers both `yours`. A session-start hook may set `QARGI_LEAD` to the session id (`qargi provider notes identity`); where none did, the incoming lead sets its own, in its environment or on each command (`QARGI_LEAD=ID qargi marshal take NAME`). A member is refused setting `QARGI_LEAD` (guard): keep that line out of its task. `--as ID` is read by the marshal verbs only: a lead that took a feature with `--as` alone is refused by `qargi feature sync` and the like, as another lead. The identity is a label, not an authority: nothing authenticates it, and no approval follows it.
- **Hooks.** A command runtime may install no qargi hook. With no guard hook, the refusals of `qargi help guard` are not made: a member is held by the runtime's own barrier (`qargi provider show RUNTIME`), and `qargi local verify` stays the check of every run. With no session-start hook, the lead reads its resources and the open features by running `qargi hook session-start` itself (it prints JSON).
- **Context and resume.** `qargi lead compact-check --advise` reads the transcript of the project's local runtime that `QARGI_LEAD` names; with none to read (no such transcript, or a runtime that names none) it prints `context: unknown` and advises nothing. A lead whose id is a label, not its session id, reads its figure with `qargi lead usage --session SESSION_ID`. A session may not be resumable: the note carries the work, never the conversation.
- **Members.** A project's members start as `qargi provider notes spawn --run RUN` says: those of a command runtime with `qargi local run`, from a lead on either runtime; those of an in-session runtime only from a session of that runtime. Changing the project's runtime is the owner's decision (`qargi-providers`).
- **Notes.** `qargi provider notes` prints the notes of the project's local runtime. An incoming lead on another runtime reads its own with `qargi provider notes --lead-runtime NAME`.
- **Skills.** A session loads its skills when it starts: a skill changed since then needs a new session, on every runtime.
- **Approvals.** No approval, plan token or owner's word transfers with a handoff. A release cut approved under your identity is refused under another: the owner approves each remaining step with its own plan.
