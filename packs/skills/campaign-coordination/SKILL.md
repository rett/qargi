# Campaign and dependency context

Apply this guidance to the supplied coordination scope. External participant projects need no registration or adoption. Declarations are planning claims, and evidence or outcome links remain unresolved pointers until separately inspected within your authority.

- In the permitted task report, record the supplied campaign identity, revision, work ID and participant identity, with their source. Record the design revision and dependency basis actually used: inspected references or pins, what you observed, and what remains assumed. Mark missing or inaccessible data `unknown`; do not invent identities or treat a declaration as observed source state.
- Attribute briefing claims to their source and revision. A recorded briefing does not establish delivery or acknowledgment. Say separately whether you observed either, and cite that observation; otherwise mark it unknown.
- When a design or dependency changes, compare the new context with the basis of your work. Identify affected assumptions, implementation and checks. Reassess provisional or stale dependencies before relying on them again. Continue only work authorized by the task whose basis remains supportable; report uncertainty and affected acceptance readiness to the lead through an approved available channel or the normal report. Do not assume peer messaging exists.
- Preserve BASE, role, scope and permission boundaries while investigating drift. Request missing context through the normal workflow; new coordination context does not expand your authority or authorize rebasing, adoption or operational effects.
- Before acceptance, require fresh review against the actual dependency as the project's standard, including necessary corrections and relevant checks. Early development permission, a settled design claim or a visible declaration commit is insufficient acceptance evidence. Record any unperformed review as outstanding.

Propose project-specific adaptations for canonical memory through the lead's normal workflow. This skill provides guidance, not mechanical enforcement, scheduling, notifications or an evidence invalidation policy.
